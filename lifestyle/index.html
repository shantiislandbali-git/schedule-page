Early Rise

Why the hour before sunrise is the most effective time for meditation and morning practice.
3 min read+20 pts

In the Vedic tradition, the window before sunrise — known as Brahma Muhurta — is considered the most favorable time for meditation and practice. The world is still, distractions are few, and the nervous system carries the quiet of sleep instead of the noise of the day.

Waking a little earlier to meet this hour, even without changing anything else about your routine, tends to make pranayama and meditation come more easily: the mind settles faster, and attention holds with less effort than later in the day.

Benefit: practicing during this window measurably deepens the focus and stillness you bring into the sits and breathwork that follow.

Morning Cleansing

Traditional cleansing before practice — one short sequence, one video.
5 min+30 pts

Scrape the tongue gently from back to front, five to seven times, rinsing after each pass. Follow with Jala Neti — a light saline rinse through each nostril — to clear the airway before pranayama. Finish with a full glass of warm water on an empty stomach.

Benefit: clears the body's surfaces before the breath work begins — the traditional preparation for pranayama.
